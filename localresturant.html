The Roots of Every Dish

Culture Behind the Food

Sikkim's cuisine is a living mosaic woven from three distinct communities — each carrying centuries of tradition onto the plate.

01

Bhutia Cuisine

The Bhutia people, of Tibetan descent, bring a cuisine shaped by high-altitude survival and Buddhist values. Their food is warming and hearty — think momos (steamed dumplings), thukpa (hand-pulled noodle broth), and Chang (fermented millet beer). Yak dairy products like Chhurpi (hard cheese) and butter tea are everyday staples, reflecting a pastoral lifestyle lived close to the Himalayan peaks.

Bhutia
02

Lepcha Cuisine

The Lepchas — Sikkim's indigenous people — are nature's foragers. Their food is intimately tied to the forest: bamboo shoots, wild ferns, fermented soybean (Kinema), and freshwater fish are the building blocks of their meals. Minimal spicing lets the earthy, natural flavours speak. Lepcha cooking is a direct conversation with the land — slow, seasonal, and deeply sustainable.

Lepcha
03

Nepali People's Cuisine

The Nepali community forms the largest group in Sikkim and their food is the heartbeat of everyday life here. Dal-Bhat-Tarkari (lentils, rice, curry) anchors every meal. Sel Roti, the crispy fried rice bread, graces every festival. Gundruk (fermented greens) and Achar (pickle) add bold, tangy depth. It is food that feeds families, fuels mountain workers, and unites communities across the hills.
